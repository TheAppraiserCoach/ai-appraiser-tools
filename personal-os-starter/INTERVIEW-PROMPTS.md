# Interview prompts — fill your files without staring at a blank page

Do not write these files by hand. Paste the prompt into any AI, answer its questions, and
have it write the file for you. Twenty minutes per file, and the result is better than
what you'd produce from scratch.

Then **read what it wrote and correct it.** It's your brain; it should sound like you.

---

## me.md

> I'm building a personal knowledge file that any AI assistant can read so it understands
> me without me re-explaining myself every time. Interview me to write it. Ask me about my
> background, how long I've done what I do, my expertise and blind spots, how I like to
> work, how I like information delivered, and what frustrates me about working with AI. Ask
> one question at a time. When you have enough, write it as clean markdown.

## business.md

> Interview me to build a reference file about my business for an AI assistant. Ask about
> what I do, who my clients are, my services and fees, my market area, my team and who does
> what, my busy and slow seasons, and the things people most often get wrong about my
> business. One question at a time, then write it as markdown.

## voice.md

> I want an AI to draft things that sound like me. Interview me about how I write and
> speak: formal or casual, long or short, words I always use, words I never use,
> punctuation habits I hate, and who I'm usually writing to. Then ask me to paste two or
> three things I've actually written so you can describe the pattern you see. Write the
> result as a style guide an AI can follow.

## goals.md

> Interview me about my current priorities so an AI can keep me pointed at them. Ask what
> I'm trying to accomplish in the next 90 days, what's actually urgent versus what just
> feels urgent, what I keep putting off, and how I'll know each one is done. Write it as a
> short prioritized list with target dates.

## principles.md

> Help me write down the rules behind my judgment calls, so an AI can make small decisions
> the way I would. Ask me about decisions I've made recently and why, what I always say yes
> to, what I always decline, and what I've learned the expensive way. Turn my answers into
> clear principles.

## processes.md

> Interview me about how I actually do my core work, step by step, including my quality
> checks and the mistakes I watch for. Ask what I do first, what I do last, where things go
> wrong, and what I check before anything leaves my desk. Write it as a workflow another
> person could follow.

## relationships.md

> Help me build a reference of the key people in my work and life, so an AI knows who I'm
> talking about. Ask who I work with most, who my clients and partners are, family members
> who come up in conversation, and anything about each that would help an AI be useful.
> Note: use first names or roles only if any of these people are confidential.

---

## Keeping it current

`second-brain.md` and `journal.md` don't get interviewed — they get *used*. Say things
like:

- "Capture this to my second brain: [thought]"
- "Log to my journal: [what we decided]"

And once a week: **"What's in my second brain that's already done or no longer relevant?"**
That's the retirement pass from `LESSONS.md`, and it's what keeps the system from becoming
a nag.
