# Welcome to {{office_name}}

Welcome aboard. This training gets you ready for your first day as a {{role_title}}. It is
six short modules, each ending in a quiz. Take your time, and don't guess — if something
here doesn't match what you're told on the job, ask {{owner_name}} rather than assuming.

Most of this training is about judgment, not procedure. The specific mechanics of how we
take an order, where files live, and which forms we use get taught hands-on in your first
week. What you can't learn by watching is the *standards* part: what you must do, what you
must never do, and when to stop and ask. That's what these modules are for.

## 1. What this office does

We appraise real property. A client — usually a lender, an appraisal management company
(AMC), an attorney, or a homeowner — hires us to give an independent, supported opinion of
value for a specific property, as of a specific date, for a specific purpose.

The product we sell is not a number. It is a **credible, defensible opinion**, documented
well enough that someone else could follow our reasoning and see how we got there. A number
by itself is worthless. A number with reasoning behind it is what a client is paying for,
and it's what protects us if the file is ever questioned years later.

## 2. Your role

As {{role_title}}, your work supports appraisal assignments. Depending on your license
status and what you're assigned, that can include:

- inspecting and measuring properties, and photographing them
- collecting and verifying property data
- researching sales, listings, and market data
- assisting with analysis and report preparation

**Work within your license level.** If you are a trainee or otherwise supervised, there are
things you may not do on your own, and there are things that must carry a supervisory
appraiser's signature. You are never in trouble for asking whether something is within
your scope. You are in trouble for guessing.

## 3. Who you'll work with

- **{{owner_name}}** — {{owner_title}}. Your supervisor and the person to escalate anything
  serious to: pressure from a client, a safety problem, a licensing issue, or a mistake you
  think matters. Reach out at {{owner_email}} or {{owner_phone}}.
- The rest of the team handles inspections, analysis, and office coordination. You'll be
  introduced in your first week.

**The escalation rule for your whole first year:** if you're unsure, ask. An hour of
someone else's time is cheaper than a bad file. Nobody here has ever been criticized for
asking a question. People do get in trouble for quietly guessing on something that mattered.

## 4. What we expect

- **Be on time and communicate.** Homeowners rearrange their day for our inspections. If
  you're running late, call ahead. If you can't make it, say so early.
- **Be accurate.** Measure carefully, photograph completely, and write down what you
  actually observed — not what you expect to be true.
- **Be professional in people's homes.** You're a guest in someone's house, often the
  biggest thing they own. Introduce yourself, be respectful, and don't comment on their
  housekeeping, their family, or their property's value.
- **Never guess on the record.** "I don't know, I'll find out" is a complete, acceptable
  answer. An invented answer in a file can follow us for years.
- **Turn work in on time.** Appraisals are deadline work. If a deadline is going to slip,
  the time to say so is when you find out, not the day it's due.

## 5. What we will never ask you to do

You will never be asked to hit a number, to change a supported conclusion because someone
is unhappy with it, or to leave something out of a report to make a client happy. If you
ever feel that's what's being asked — by anyone, including someone at this office — say so.
Module 4 covers exactly what to do.
