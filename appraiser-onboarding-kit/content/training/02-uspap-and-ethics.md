# USPAP and Professional Ethics

USPAP — the **Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice** — is the rulebook for
appraisal work in the United States. It is not optional, it is not a guideline, and it is
not something you comply with only when a client asks. It is enforced by your state board,
and violating it can cost you your license.

This module covers the parts you will run into constantly. It is not a substitute for the
USPAP course you take for licensing, and USPAP is periodically updated — the version in
effect at the time of the assignment is the one that governs.

## 1. What USPAP actually is

USPAP is published by the Appraisal Standards Board. It sets out **Rules** (Ethics,
Competency, Scope of Work, Jurisdictional Exception, Record Keeping) and **Standards**
governing how appraisals are developed and reported.

The through-line of all of it: an appraisal must be **independent, impartial, objective,
and credible**, and there must be a record showing it was.

## 2. The Ethics Rule

The Ethics Rule has four parts, and you should know all four by name.

**Conduct.** You must perform assignments with impartiality, objectivity, and independence,
and without accommodating personal interests. You must not act in a misleading or fraudulent
way, or knowingly communicate a misleading report. You must not use or rely on unsupported
conclusions about protected characteristics — race, color, religion, national origin, sex,
gender identity, sexual orientation, familial status, age, disability — or presume anything
about a neighborhood based on them.

**Management.** You must disclose any prior services on the property, any interest you have
in it, and any relationship with parties involved. You must not accept an assignment fee
that is **contingent** on reporting a predetermined value, a direction in value, the amount
of a value opinion, whether a loan closes, or anything else favorable to a party. Paying or
accepting undisclosed fees for getting work is prohibited.

**Confidentiality.** You must protect confidential information and assignment results. This
gets its own module (Module 3) because it comes up daily.

**Record Keeping.** You must prepare a workfile for each assignment and keep it. See
Section 5 below.

## 3. The Competency Rule

Before accepting an assignment, you must have the knowledge and experience to complete it
competently — or you must take steps to get there.

If you're not competent for an assignment, you have three obligations:

1. **Disclose** your lack of knowledge or experience *before* accepting.
2. **Take the steps necessary** to become competent — study, or associate with someone who
   has the expertise.
3. **Describe** in the report the lack of knowledge or experience and the steps you took.

The honest version: a complex rural property with outbuildings, an unusual easement, or a
property type you've never handled is not a "figure it out as you go" assignment. Say so
up front. Competency is a disclosure requirement, not an admission of failure.

## 4. Scope of Work

The **Scope of Work Rule** requires that the work you do is enough to produce credible
results *for the intended use*. You must identify the problem to be solved, determine and
perform a scope of work that is acceptable, and disclose the scope of work in the report.

The scope is driven by the assignment, not by the fee and not by the deadline. If the fee
or timeline doesn't allow the work an assignment actually requires, the answer is to
decline the assignment or renegotiate it — never to quietly do less work and report it as
though you did more.

## 5. The Record Keeping Rule and workfiles

You must prepare a **workfile** for every assignment. It has to contain enough for someone
to understand what you did: the identification of the property and the client, the intended
use and users, the data you relied on, your analyses, and a copy of the report you
delivered.

**Retention:** at least **five years** after preparation, or at least **two years** after
final disposition of any judicial proceeding in which you gave testimony — whichever is
longer.

At this office, workfiles are stored where {{owner_name}} directs, not on your personal
device or your personal cloud account, and they are never deleted by you. Assume every file
could be pulled and examined years from now, because some of them will be.

## 6. What "credible" means

Credible means **worthy of belief** — and it's judged against the intended use of the
appraisal, not against your confidence in it. A conclusion is credible when the data and
the analysis actually support it and a reader can follow the path.

The practical test to apply to your own work: *if a state board investigator read this file
in three years with no help from me, would they be able to see why I concluded what I
concluded?* If the answer is no, the file isn't done.

# USPAP and Ethics Quiz (6 questions)

**Q1. Which of these fee arrangements is prohibited under the Ethics Rule?**
- A) A flat fee agreed to before the assignment
- B) An hourly fee for consulting work
- C) ✅ A fee contingent on the appraisal reaching a specific value or on a loan closing
- D) A higher fee for a complex property that requires more work

**Q2. You're offered an assignment on a property type you've never appraised. What does the Competency Rule require?**
- A) Decline it — you can't legally accept it
- B) Accept it and do your best, then note any uncertainty
- C) ✅ Disclose the lack of experience before accepting, take steps to become competent, and describe those steps in the report
- D) Accept it and have someone else sign the report

**Q3. How long must a workfile be retained?**
- A) Two years from preparation, always
- B) ✅ At least 5 years after preparation, or at least 2 years after final disposition of any judicial proceeding in which you testified — whichever is longer
- C) Until the loan closes
- D) Seven years, matching IRS record requirements

**Q4. The fee and deadline on an assignment don't allow for the work it really requires. What's the correct response?**
- A) Do a lighter scope of work and report it normally
- B) Do the reduced work and note the fee was low
- C) ✅ Decline or renegotiate the assignment — scope is driven by the assignment, never by the fee or the deadline
- D) Complete it and flag it to the state board

**Q5. Which is a violation of the Conduct section of the Ethics Rule?**
- A) Declining an assignment outside your competency
- B) Disclosing a prior service on the property
- C) Describing a neighborhood using verified market data
- D) ✅ Making an assumption about a neighborhood based on the race or national origin of the people who live there

**Q6. What is the practical test for whether your file is "credible"?**
- A) Whether the client accepts it without questions
- B) Whether the value came in near the contract price
- C) ✅ Whether someone reading the file years later, with no help from you, could follow how you reached your conclusion
- D) Whether it was completed on time
