# Getting Paid: Reporting Your Time

Your work is only half the job. Reporting your time correctly and on time is how you make
sure you actually get paid on time. This is the process, and it doesn't change. Learn it
once and you'll never miss a check.

## 1. The schedule

**Payday is {{pay_cadence}}.** That day is both payday and the deadline for submitting
your time.

- **Submit your hours by {{time_deadline}}.** Set a repeating reminder on your phone so
  you never have to remember it.
- **Late hours can cost you a full cycle.** If your time comes in after the cutoff, we
  can't promise it gets processed that period, and it may have to wait until the next
  payday. Don't let that happen to you.
- **Payment arrives {{pay_lag}} after submission.**

## 2. How to submit

{{time_method}}

Include a legible copy of your logged time — a screenshot from a time-tracking app or a
clear photo of a handwritten log both work. If we can't read it, we can't pay it.

**Put your total hours in decimal format**, not hours and minutes. Minutes divided by 60:
**15 minutes = .25**, **30 minutes = .5**, **45 minutes = .75**. So 43 hours and 30 minutes
is **43.5 hours**, and 40 hours and 15 minutes is **40.25 hours**.

## 3. Record all the time you actually work

This matters legally, not just administratively.

- **Record everything you work.** Drive time between assignments, time spent on research
  at home, a call you took after hours — if you worked it, it goes on the sheet.
- **Never work "off the clock."** Not to look efficient, not to finish something, not
  because you think the hours look high. If you worked it, report it, and the Firm will
  pay it. Working unrecorded hours is a problem for both of us.
- **Never falsify time.** Padding a timesheet is grounds for immediate termination, and
  it's the fastest way to end a career in a small industry where everyone knows everyone.
- If you're unsure whether something counts as work time, ask {{owner_name}} rather than
  guessing in either direction.

## 4. Expenses

Job-related expenses — mileage, county records fees, MLS access, parking — are submitted
with your time, with receipts where you have them. Ask {{owner_name}} what this office
reimburses before you assume something is covered.

## 5. If something's wrong

If you don't hear back within a couple of hours of submitting, follow up at
{{owner_phone}}. Emails get lost, and we'd rather hear from you twice than have you go
unpaid.

If a check is wrong — hours missing, the rate looks off, an expense wasn't included — say
so right away. Payroll mistakes are easy to fix in the current cycle and annoying to
untangle three cycles later.
