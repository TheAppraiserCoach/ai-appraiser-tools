This Employment Agreement ("Agreement") is between **{{office_name}}** ("the Firm"), of
{{city_state}}, and **{{full_name}}** ("you"), effective **{{start_date}}**.

## 1. Your position

You are employed as **{{role_title}}**. You report to {{supervisor_name}}. Your duties
include the work assigned to you by the Firm in connection with real property appraisal
and related services, which may include property inspection, data collection,
photography, research, analysis, and report preparation, as directed and as permitted by
your license status.

You agree to perform your work competently, honestly, and on schedule, and to keep the
Firm informed about the status of your assignments.

## 2. At-will employment **[CONFIRM]**

Your employment is **at will**. That means either you or the Firm may end the employment
relationship at any time, with or without cause and with or without notice. Nothing in
this Agreement, in any handbook, or in anything said to you creates a contract of
employment for any specific length of time. No manager or owner of the Firm can change
this except in a writing signed by the owner.

## 3. Compensation

Your pay is as stated in your offer and as communicated to you separately in writing. Pay
is subject to all lawful withholdings and deductions, including federal and state taxes.

You must accurately record all time you work. Falsifying time records is grounds for
immediate termination. If you are classified as non-exempt, you will be paid overtime as
required by law, and you must not perform work "off the clock" — if you work it, record
it, and the Firm will pay it.

## 4. Licensing and continuing education

If your role requires an appraiser license, certification, or trainee registration, you
must hold it, keep it current and in good standing, and complete all required continuing
education on time.

You must notify the Firm **immediately** if any of the following happens:

- your license or registration lapses, is suspended, or is revoked
- you are the subject of a state board complaint, investigation, or disciplinary action
- you are named in a lawsuit or claim related to appraisal work
- you are placed on any exclusionary or ineligible appraiser list by any client, AMC,
  lender, or agency

These are not automatically grounds for discipline by the Firm, but the Firm has to know
about them, and it has to hear about them from you first.

## 5. USPAP and professional standards

All appraisal work you perform through the Firm must comply with the **Uniform Standards
of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP)** in effect at the time of the assignment, and
with all applicable federal and state law, including the Fair Housing Act and the Equal
Credit Opportunity Act.

You will not:

- state a predetermined value, or accept an assignment conditioned on reporting a
  predetermined value or a minimum valuation
- accept a fee contingent on the value reached, on a loan closing, or on any other
  outcome favorable to a party
- include, or rely on, any statement or assumption based on race, color, religion, sex,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, familial status, national origin, disability, or
  any other protected characteristic of anyone connected to the property or the
  neighborhood
- perform an assignment you are not competent to perform, unless you disclose that and
  take the steps USPAP's Competency Rule requires

If you are a trainee or supervised appraiser, you will work within the scope your license
level allows and under the supervision arrangement required by your state.

## 6. Appraiser independence

Federal law and USPAP protect your independence, and the Firm expects you to use it.

If anyone — a client, an AMC, a lender, a loan officer, a real estate agent, a borrower,
a homeowner, or anyone at the Firm — pressures you to reach a particular value, to change
a conclusion you believe is correct, or to omit something you believe belongs in the
report, you will:

1. Not change your opinion because of the pressure.
2. Document what was said, by whom, and when.
3. Report it to {{owner_name}} promptly.

The Firm will not retaliate against you for refusing to change a value, for reporting
pressure, or for reporting a suspected violation of law or USPAP. **[CONFIRM]**

## 7. Confidentiality and client data

In this job you will handle confidential information: appraisal files and workfiles,
client instructions, fee arrangements, borrower and homeowner personal information,
property access details, and the Firm's business information.

You agree that:

- **Assignment results and confidential information are confidential.** You will not
  disclose them to anyone other than the client, parties the client authorizes, and
  people at the Firm who need to know, except where USPAP or law requires disclosure.
- **Borrower and homeowner personal information is sensitive.** Names, contact details,
  financial information, and anything else nonpublic stays protected. Do not email it
  unencrypted to people who don't need it, do not store it on personal devices or personal
  cloud accounts, and do not leave printed files where others can see them.
- **You will not discuss specific assignments publicly**, including on social media, and
  you will not use identifiable property photos or client information in personal
  marketing.
- These obligations continue after your employment ends.

## 8. Workfiles and work product

Every appraisal you prepare through the Firm, and every workfile supporting it, is the
property of the Firm, along with the data, photographs, sketches, research, notes, and
files you create in the course of your work.

You will prepare and maintain a workfile for each assignment that meets USPAP's Record
Keeping Rule, and you will store it where the Firm directs so the Firm can meet its
retention obligation. **You will not delete or remove workfiles**, and when your
employment ends you will leave all files, data, and equipment with the Firm.

USPAP requires workfiles to be retained for at least five years after preparation, or at
least two years after final disposition of any judicial proceeding in which you testified,
whichever is longer. Assume every file matters.

## 9. Equipment and systems

The Firm may provide equipment, software, and accounts for your work. You will use them
for Firm business, keep them secure, use unique passwords, and return everything on
request or when your employment ends. Do not install unauthorized software on Firm
equipment and do not share your account credentials.

## 10. Errors and omissions insurance **[CONFIRM]**

Your coverage arrangement — whether you are covered under the Firm's errors and omissions
policy, or you carry your own — is as stated in your offer. If you are required to carry
your own, you will maintain it continuously and give the Firm a certificate of insurance
on request.

## 11. Conduct and harassment

The Firm expects professional conduct with clients, homeowners, agents, and coworkers,
including in people's homes.

Harassment and discrimination are not tolerated — not by employees, not by clients, not by
anyone the Firm does business with. This includes conduct based on any protected
characteristic, and it includes sexual harassment. If you experience or witness it, report
it to {{owner_name}}. Reports will be taken seriously, and the Firm will not retaliate
against anyone for making one in good faith.

You are frequently in other people's homes, sometimes with a homeowner present and
sometimes alone. Behave accordingly: identify yourself, respect the property, photograph
only what the assignment requires, and leave if you feel unsafe.

## 12. Outside appraisal work **[CONFIRM]**

You will not perform appraisal work outside the Firm that conflicts with your duties here
or with the Firm's client relationships without discussing it with {{owner_name}} first.
This is not a general ban on other employment.

## 13. Non-solicitation **[CONFIRM]**

For **one (1) year** after your employment ends, you will not solicit the Firm's clients
that you worked with or learned about during your employment, and you will not solicit the
Firm's employees or contractors to leave.

*Note to the office owner: this is a non-solicitation clause, not a non-compete. Non-competes
are unenforceable in some states and increasingly disfavored generally. Ask your attorney
what is enforceable where you are before relying on this.*

- **Entire agreement:** this Agreement is the entire agreement between you and the Firm on
  these subjects and replaces any prior understanding.
- **Changes:** only in a writing signed by both you and the Firm.
- **Severability:** if a court finds any part unenforceable, the rest still applies.
- **Not a guarantee of employment:** see Section 2.
