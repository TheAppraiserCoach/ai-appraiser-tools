# Independent Contractor Agreement — Appraisal Services

> **OWNER'S NOTE — READ BEFORE FIRST USE (this note never appears in the signed document):**
> This template was written for a small US residential appraisal office engaging an
> independent contractor. It is **not legal advice**, no attorney has reviewed it, and no
> one connected with this kit is your lawyer. You are choosing the legal terms your office
> operates under; whether to have your own attorney review this is **your decision and
> your responsibility**. If you do get it reviewed, set `legal.reviewed_by_attorney` to
> true in your config to silence the setup reminder.
>
> **The classification warning is the important one.** Signing a paper that says
> "independent contractor" does not make someone a contractor. The IRS, the Department of
> Labor, and your state agency look at what actually happens: who sets the hours, who
> controls how the work is done, who supplies the equipment, whether the person can work
> for others, whether they can profit or lose money on the engagement. If you set someone's
> schedule, tell them how to do the work, and they only work for you, you very likely have
> an **employee**, and using this agreement instead of the employment one is the expensive
> kind of mistake — back taxes, penalties, and unpaid overtime. Some states (California and
> others) apply a stricter test than the IRS does. When it is close, ask your accountant
> or attorney before you send this, not after.
>
> Sections marked **[CONFIRM]** are the state-sensitive or office-specific ones. The
> markers are stripped from the signed document either way.
>
> This same agreement is used by the **Foreign Contractor** track. For a contractor who is
> not a US person and performs the work outside the US, the tax form is a **W-8BEN**, not a
> W-9 — Section 4 covers both. Cross-border engagements have their own rules; get advice
> before you rely on this for one.

This Independent Contractor Agreement ("Agreement") is between **{{office_name}}** ("the

## 1. What you are, and what you are not

You are an **independent contractor**. You are not an employee, partner, agent, or joint
venturer of the Firm, and this Agreement does not create an employment relationship.

That distinction is not a formality — it decides how you are paid and taxed:

- The Firm does **not** withhold income tax, Social Security, or Medicare from your pay,
  and does not pay the employer share of those taxes for you.
- You are **not** eligible for employee benefits: no health insurance, no paid time off, no
  retirement plan, no workers' compensation coverage through the Firm, and no unemployment
  insurance based on this engagement. **[CONFIRM]**
- You are responsible for your own self-employment taxes and any estimated tax payments.

## 2. Control over your own work

You control the manner and means of your work. The Firm tells you **what** the assignment
requires and when it is due; **how** you get it done is yours.

- You set your own hours and your own schedule, subject only to assignment due dates and
  to appointments you arrange with property contacts.
- You supply your own equipment, software, vehicle, phone, and tools, unless the Firm
  agrees in writing to provide something specific.
- You may work for other clients, including other appraisal firms, subject to Section 9.
- The Firm may direct the **result** — a complete, USPAP-compliant assignment delivered on
  time — but does not direct your day-to-day methods.

You may not subcontract an assignment to anyone else without the Firm's written consent.

## 3. Assignments and acceptance

Each assignment is offered individually and separately. Neither side is obligated: the
Firm is not required to offer you any minimum amount of work, and **you are free to decline
any assignment** without explanation and without affecting future offers.

Before accepting an assignment, confirm you are competent to perform it and that you have
no conflict of interest in it. If you accept an assignment and then cannot complete it,
tell {{owner_name}} immediately — a silent late file is worse than a declined one.

## 4. Fees, invoicing, and taxes

You are paid a **fee per assignment**, as agreed in writing before the assignment begins.
Nothing in this Agreement guarantees any amount of work or income.

- **Invoicing.** {{invoice_method}}
- **Invoice deadline.** {{invoice_deadline}}
- **Payment timing.** The Firm's payment run is {{invoice_cadence}}, and payment on a
  correct invoice arrives {{invoice_pay_lag}}. **[CONFIRM]**
- **Tax form.** Before your first payment you must provide a completed **W-9** (or a
  **W-8BEN** if you are not a US person). The Firm cannot pay you without it. If the Firm
  pays you $600 or more in a calendar year, you will receive a **1099-NEC**.
- **Expenses.** Your business expenses — vehicle, mileage, phone, software, insurance,
  license fees, continuing education — are **yours**, not the Firm's, unless the Firm
  agrees in writing in advance to reimburse a specific cost. **[CONFIRM]**
- **Fees are not contingent.** No fee under this Agreement is or may be made contingent on
  a value reached, on a loan closing, or on any other outcome favorable to any party.

Invoice accurately. Billing for work not performed ends the relationship and, depending on
what was billed to whom, is fraud.

## 5. USPAP and professional standards

All appraisal work you perform for or through the Firm must comply with the **Uniform
Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP)** in effect at the time of the
assignment, and with all applicable federal and state law, including the Fair Housing Act
and the Equal Credit Opportunity Act.

You will not:

- state a predetermined value, or accept an assignment conditioned on reporting a
  predetermined value or a minimum valuation
- accept a fee contingent on the value reached, on a loan closing, or on any other outcome
  favorable to a party
- include, or rely on, any statement or assumption based on race, color, religion, sex,
  gender identity, sexual orientation, familial status, national origin, disability, or any
  other protected characteristic of anyone connected to the property or the neighborhood
- perform an assignment you are not competent to perform, unless you disclose that and take
  the steps USPAP's Competency Rule requires

Being a contractor does not lower this standard. It raises your personal exposure, because
the signature on the report is yours.

## 6. Licensing, insurance, and your own compliance

You must hold, and keep current and in good standing, every license, certification, or
trainee registration your work requires. Continuing education is your responsibility and
your expense.

**You carry your own errors and omissions insurance.** The Firm's policy does not cover
you, and you will provide a certificate of insurance on request. **[CONFIRM]**

You must notify the Firm **immediately** if:

- your license or registration lapses, is suspended, or is revoked
- you are the subject of a state board complaint, investigation, or disciplinary action
- you are named in a lawsuit or claim related to appraisal work
- you are placed on any exclusionary or ineligible appraiser list by any client, AMC,
  lender, or agency

The Firm has to know, and it has to hear it from you first.

## 7. Appraiser independence

Federal law and USPAP protect your independence, and the Firm expects you to use it.

If anyone — a client, an AMC, a lender, a loan officer, a real estate agent, a borrower, a
homeowner, or anyone at the Firm — pressures you to reach a particular value, to change a
conclusion you believe is correct, or to omit something you believe belongs in the report,
you will:

1. Not change your opinion because of the pressure.
2. Document what was said, by whom, and when.
3. Report it to {{owner_name}} promptly.

The Firm will not retaliate against you, and will not withhold future assignments, for
refusing to change a value or for reporting pressure or a suspected violation. **[CONFIRM]**

## 8. Confidentiality, workfiles, and work product

In this work you will handle confidential information: appraisal files and workfiles,
client instructions, fee arrangements, borrower and homeowner personal information,
property access details, and the Firm's business information.

You agree that:

- **Assignment results and confidential information are confidential.** You will not
  disclose them to anyone other than the client, parties the client authorizes, and people
  at the Firm who need to know, except where USPAP or law requires disclosure.
- **Borrower and homeowner personal information is sensitive.** Do not email it
  unencrypted to people who don't need it, do not store it in personal cloud accounts, and
  do not leave printed files where others can see them.
- **You will not discuss specific assignments publicly**, including on social media, and
  you will not use identifiable property photos or client information in your own
  marketing.
- These obligations continue after this Agreement ends.

**Work product.** Reports and deliverables you prepare for the Firm's clients under this
Agreement, and the data, photographs, sketches, and research created for them, belong to
the Firm on payment, and you assign to the Firm the rights needed for the Firm to deliver
and support them.

**Workfiles.** USPAP makes the appraiser who signs the report responsible for the workfile.
You will prepare and maintain a workfile for each assignment that meets USPAP's Record
Keeping Rule — at least five years after preparation, or two years after final disposition
of any judicial proceeding in which you testified, whichever is longer — and you will give
the Firm a complete copy, in the format the Firm directs, so the Firm can meet its own
retention obligation. Neither of you may delete a workfile inside that window. **[CONFIRM]**

## 9. Conflicts and other clients

You may work for other clients. You will not, however:

- accept an assignment that conflicts with an assignment you are performing for the Firm
- use the Firm's confidential information, client list, or workfiles for other clients
- accept work directly from one of the Firm's clients on an assignment the Firm referred to
  you or that you learned about through the Firm, for **one (1) year** after this Agreement
  ends, without the Firm's written consent **[CONFIRM]**

*Note to the office owner: this is a narrow non-solicitation, not a non-compete. Non-competes
against independent contractors are unenforceable in some states and increasingly disfavored
generally, and an aggressive one can itself be evidence of an employment relationship. Ask
your attorney what is enforceable where you are.*

## 10. Conduct in the field

You are frequently in other people's homes, sometimes with a homeowner present and
sometimes alone. Identify yourself, respect the property, photograph only what the
assignment requires, and leave if you feel unsafe.

Harassment and discrimination have no place in this work — toward homeowners, agents,
clients, or anyone at the Firm. If you experience or witness it on a Firm assignment,
report it to {{owner_name}}.

## 11. Term and termination

This Agreement continues until either side ends it. **Either you or the Firm may end it at
any time, with or without cause, on written notice.** Assignments already accepted and in
progress should be completed unless both sides agree otherwise; the Firm pays for completed
work, and you deliver the workfiles for it.

Sections 4 (tax form and records), 8 (confidentiality, work product, workfiles), 9
(conflicts), and 12 (indemnity) survive termination.

## 12. Indemnity **[CONFIRM]**

You are responsible for your own work. You will indemnify the Firm against claims,
damages, and reasonable costs arising from your negligence, your willful misconduct, your
breach of this Agreement, or a determination that you failed to pay taxes owed on amounts
paid to you under it. The Firm is responsible for its own acts on the same terms.
