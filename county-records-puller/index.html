County Records Puller

Give it a subject address. Claude opens your county's own websites in your browser and pulls the assessor record, tax card, GIS map, plat, legal description, and ownership history into one subject data sheet, with a source link for every figure.

← All tools

1What this is

Starting a report usually means ten browser tabs: the assessor, the treasurer, the GIS viewer, the recorder, the plat. This prompt hands that job to Claude in Chrome, which works inside your own browser. It finds the county, goes to the county's public sites, and collects the subject data for you.

It works in any county. It doesn't use a pre-built scraper that breaks when a county redesigns its site. Claude reads the page the way you would. If a site shows a CAPTCHA, a login, or a paywall, it stops and asks you to handle it, then continues.

It reads and reports only. It never buys a document, submits a form, or changes anything on a county site.

2One-time setup

  1. Use Google Chrome on a computer (not a phone).
  2. Install the Claude extension. Go to claude.com/claude-in-chrome, add it to Chrome, and sign in with your Claude account. A paid Claude plan is required.
  3. Pin it. Click the puzzle-piece icon in Chrome's toolbar and pin Claude so it's one click away.
  4. Optional, but it saves the most time: if your county's data sits behind a login (a paid parcel viewer or a recorder account), sign in to that site in Chrome first. Claude uses the session you already have. It never sees or asks for your password.

3How to use it

  1. Open the Claude side panel in Chrome.
  2. Paste the prompt you copied above.
  3. Add the subject address at the bottom, for example: Subject: 123 Main St, Rigby, ID 83442. If you already have the parcel number (APN) from the engagement letter, add it too. It makes the search much faster.
  4. Let it work. Watch the tabs open. If it stops at a CAPTCHA or login, handle it and tell Claude "continue."
  5. Review the data sheet. It comes back with every field, the source link for each, and a list of anything it couldn't find or that conflicts between sources.

Tip: the first run in a new county takes longest, because Claude has to find the county's sites. At the end it gives you a short "county cheat sheet" of the exact links it used. Save it, and paste it in with the address next time to go straight to the right pages.

4What you get back

  • Identification: owner of record, owner mailing address, parcel number / APN, county, legal description, subdivision, lot and block, and the tax district
  • Site: lot size (acres and square feet), zoning, flood zone if the GIS shows it
  • Improvements (per the assessor): year built, living area, basement, bedrooms and bathrooms, style, garage, and outbuildings
  • Assessment and taxes: land, improvement, and total values; tax year; annual tax; exemptions; any delinquency shown
  • Ownership and sales history: prior owners, sale or deed dates, prices where the state discloses them, and document numbers (at least 3 years back, which is the USPAP minimum for the subject)
  • Maps and documents: links to (and, where your browser allows, saved copies of) the GIS parcel map, the plat map, and the assessor property card
  • A flag list: fields it couldn't find, sources that disagree, and assessor data that looks stale

Some states, including Idaho, Texas, and Utah, don't publish sale prices in public records. Claude records the transfer and deed date, and marks the price "not disclosed by this state." Get the price from MLS or the parties.
